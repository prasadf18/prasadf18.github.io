---
title: "A Year in Review: From Breadth to Depth"
date: 2026-11-09
---

A year ago someone suggested I start writing. I put it off. I told myself I had little to say, and I spent more time thinking about it than doing it. This post is my reflection on the past year and what I'm focusing on next.

## What I worked on

I started as an intern and spent two years working as a developer, delivering the work in front of me. Alongside that, I built skills across the stack: React, Python and Node.js, and then AI engineering, where I built an AI agent with voice. I was also recognized with an award at work, which I'm grateful for.

## What I learned

**Overthinking slows you down.** I planned and researched more than I shipped. The blog is a small example: one simple task that waited a year. The lesson is to start small and iterate.

**Breadth is not depth.** Every week brings a new tool or framework, and I tried to keep up with all of it. I ended up with wide exposure and not enough depth in any one area.

**The internet is noisy.** Everyone has an opinion on what to learn and what to build. Consuming more of it does not make you the kind of engineer people want to work with. Depth, judgment and delivering things do.

**Knowing where the gaps are is useful.** I ran into technical questions I couldn't answer in depth. I could use the tools, but I couldn't always explain how they worked one level down. That is a depth problem, and it is fixable.

## What's next

I'm going to pick one area and go deep. For me that's databases, distributed systems and building AI agents. I'm not tied to one language. I'll use Go, Python, TypeScript or whatever stack fits the problem.

My next goal is to build and ship small apps on my own time, try indie hacking, and share what I learn as I go, including what doesn't work.

I'll treat every question I can't answer as a study list: write down what I got stuck on and learn exactly that.

I'll keep growing in my current role by taking on harder problems and bringing back what I learn.

And I'll keep a sustainable pace, because a smaller plan I follow beats a big one I drop.

## Why I'm sharing this

Writing it down keeps me accountable. If you've worked on moving from breadth to depth, I'd like to hear what helped you.
