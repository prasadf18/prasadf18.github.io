---
title: "Year of Downfall"
date: 2026-11-09
---

A year ago someone told me to write a blog. I nodded and did nothing. I told myself I had nothing worth saying. The truth is I was busy overthinking, and writing felt like one more thing I could do badly.

So this is the honest version of my last year.

## How I spent it

I worked. I started as an intern, and then I spent two years doing the job in front of me. I also tried to learn on the side, and I did learn a lot. React, Python, Node.js, working across the stack. Then AI engineering, where I built an AI agent, a voice AI one. I also won an award at work, because I worked hard and it got noticed.

From the outside that sounds like a good year. And in some ways it was. But I felt like I was spreading myself thin.

## What went wrong

I overthought and I under acted. I would plan something, read about it, compare myself to people who were way ahead, and then not start. The blog is the best example. One small thing, and it sat for a year.

I also tried to do everything. Every week there was a new tool, a new framework, a new thing I was supposed to know. So I touched a lot of things and went deep into none of them.

I let the fear of being behind make my decisions. It wasn't really about AI taking my job. It was the competition and the chaos. The internet is already crowded, and everyone has an opinion on what you should learn and what you should build. Everybody is shouting, and a lot of it is noise. I kept learning one thing after another to keep up, but learning something and becoming the engineer people actually want to work with are two different things. I was doing the first and mixing it up with the second.

Then I ran into technical questions I couldn't answer well. That hurt, but it also taught me something. I knew a lot of things on the surface and not enough underneath. I could use tools, but I couldn't explain how they worked when someone asked me to go one level deeper. That is a depth problem, and it is fixable.

## What I want to change

Pick one area and go deep. For me that's databases, distributed systems and building AI agents, with Go as my main language. Everything else can wait.

Build things and share them while I build. Small, imperfect, public. I'd rather show something half finished than keep polishing something nobody sees.

Treat every question I can't answer as a study list. Write down what I got stuck on, and learn exactly that.

Grow where I am. Take on harder problems at work, go deeper on what I'm already using, and bring back what I learn.

And rest more. I can't build anything when I'm exhausted. A small plan I follow is better than a huge one I drop.

## Why I'm posting this

Mostly so I have to follow through. If it's written down where people can see it, I'm more likely to do it. And maybe someone else has had a year like this, busy and stuck at the same time.

I don't have it figured out. But I'd rather figure it out in the open than keep planning in private. If you've been through something like this, tell me what got you moving.
